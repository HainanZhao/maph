# Research ideas

This is a durable holding file for promising directions that are not yet
part of an active project plan. Entries are hypotheses and research
programs, not theorem claims. Before promotion, each needs a literature
audit, an exact statement, and the usual certificate/proof gate.

## 2026-07-30 — Stark phases, recognition, genus constraints, and index two

### 1. Phase quantization for quartic rank-one Stark units

Roblot's Theorem 6.1 constructs a canonical weak solution \(\eta\), unique
up to the action of \(\{\pm1\}G\). For a quartic character \(\chi\), put

\[
  c(\eta)=\frac12\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)\log|\eta^g|.
\]

The trivial ambiguity acts on \(c\) through the fourth roots of unity, so
its orbit is

\[
  \{c,-c,ic,-ic\}.
\]

Roblot's weak theorem supplies the radial identity

\[
  |L'(0,\chi)|=|c|.
\]

The full Stark identity asks for the discrete refinement

\[
  L'(0,\chi)\in\{c,-c,ic,-ic\}.
\]

Equivalently, the phase defect

\[
  \arg L'(0,\chi)-\arg c
\]

must vanish modulo \(\pi/2\). Thus, in the cyclic-quartic rank-one
stratum, the gap between Roblot's unconditional index-formula solution
and the full Stark unit may be exactly one quantized phase equation.

Two possible outputs follow.

1. **Retroactive certification corollary.** Check the hypotheses and
   conventions case by case for the five certified CM-quartic packets in
   the Effective-Stark results. Where they lie in Roblot's exact setting,
   the componentwise packet proof should identify his index-formula
   solution with the Stark unit, rather than merely with an
   absolute-value candidate. This may give the first explicit cases with
   \(m\ge2\) in which Roblot's constructed solutions are proved to be
   the actual Stark units.
2. **Phase-defect census.** For quartic higher-support rows with a
   Roblot-canonical algebraic comparison object, compute
   \(\arg L'(0,\chi)-\arg c\pmod{\pi/2}\). Stark predicts zero. This would
   be stronger numerical evidence than recognition against a discovered
   algebraic candidate because the comparison object is unconditional
   and canonical before the analytic computation.

Before claiming novelty, search Roblot's footnote 3 and downstream
citations, Popescu's Rubin--Stark surveys, and Stark's sign/phase
discussions in Chapter IV. Current novelty estimate: high but not yet
certified; the reformulation may be implicit in expert knowledge.

### 2. Roblot-canonical recognition for quartic Engine-B cases

The present Engine-B recognition step finds an algebraic candidate and
then resolves equality through an enclosure small enough for a
Voutier-height comparison, often at scale \(V_{\min}/m\).

For quartic support, Roblot's theorem may supply the candidate
unconditionally up to the explicit four-element phase orbit above.
Recognition would then require only enough analytic precision to
separate four known algebraic alternatives. Their pairwise separations
are exactly computable and should usually be much larger than the
generic Voutier floor. This could reduce the required precision by
orders of magnitude and move high-conductor quartic cases below the
per-case resource cap.

This does **not** remove Shintani/Stark algebraicity from the proof. It
only replaces generic algebraic recognition by canonical finite-orbit
selection. Treat this as a practical improvement to Engine B, not as an
independent algebraicity theorem.

Suggested test: select one quartic case currently near or beyond the
resource cap, preregister the four candidates and their exact minimum
separation, and compare the resulting precision budget with the
Voutier budget.

### 3. Genus constraints on the Stark sub-packet consumed by the TCC

For the AFK characteristic map, a characteristic \((p,q)\) maps to the
class of the ideal generated by \(q\beta-\widetilde p\), whose norm is a
value of the corresponding AFK form \(Q\). Values represented by a
fixed form lie in fixed genus cosets. Therefore the image of the
characteristic map should be genus-constrained, and the TCC may consume
only a proper sub-packet of the full ray-class Stark packet.

The exact finite question is:

> Which subgroup is generated by the nonzero characteristic classes,
> and what is the order profile of the Fourier support after restriction
> to that subgroup?

In dimension five the restriction is vacuous: the recorded
\(q\)-coordinate table generates all of \(C_8\). The important tests are
composite dimensions with large ray-group \(2\)-rank, such as the
dimension-64 example with group
\(C_{64}\times C_{16}\times C_2\times C_2\) and 4095 nonzero
characteristics.

If the characteristic image factors through a 2-elementary quotient,
the quadratic Engine-A theorem could prove a TCC input whose ambient
ray group suggests much higher-order support. A negative result would
still be a structural theorem about the characteristic map.

First experiment: compute the exact generated subgroup and restricted
character orders for the frozen dimension-64 case, then repeat across
the existing admissible-dimension corpus. Keep the distinction between
the ambient packet and the TCC-consumed sub-packet explicit.

### 4. A genus-theoretic formula for Shintani index-two eligibility

The census currently determines

\[
  [H:H\cap\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}]
\]

through genuine normal-closure and maximal-abelian-subfield
computations. The maximal absolutely abelian subfield of a ray class
field is naturally a ray-genus-field object. Furuta-style genus theory
therefore suggests an exact formula, or at least a finite arithmetic
criterion, in terms of the real-quadratic discriminant, the finite
modulus, local ramification, and unit images.

A successful criterion would:

1. replace the expensive Layer-2 normal-closure screen by exact local
   and class-group arithmetic;
2. explain the observed index distribution structurally;
3. make the density of index-two, Shintani-eligible moduli accessible
   to analytic study.

The main research question is:

> Among one-place moduli over real quadratic fields, what proportion
> admits any currently known unconditional identification mechanism?

This direction is likely to assemble known genus-field ingredients
into a new effective criterion rather than prove every component from
scratch. The density question is the potentially new theorem.

### Suggested priority

1. Write the phase-quantization note and verify the retroactive
   Roblot-solution corollary against the five CM case bundles.
2. Run the dimension-64 characteristic-image/genus experiment.
3. Use one high-conductor quartic case to measure the canonical-orbit
   precision reduction.
4. Develop the ray-genus-field index formula after a focused Furuta and
   ray-genus literature review.

The first item is the most time-sensitive: it is short,
self-contained, and becomes visually apparent once the Effective-Stark
results and Roblot's theorem are read side by side.

## 2026-07-30 — High-risk cross-domain collisions

These are deliberately wilder than the directions above. Each combines
machinery or communities that are not normally placed in the same
argument. None is a theorem claim. Before promotion, the asserted
boundary coincidences and prior-art gaps require primary-source
audits. The point of retaining them is that every proposal has a
bounded computation capable of producing useful negative evidence
within one research week.

### 5. The provability wall and the crystallographic restriction

Place the known proof mechanisms beside one another:

- quadratic methods close support order two;
- cyclic-quartic CM descent naturally sees order four;
- Roblot's index method treats the quadratic, cyclic-quartic, and,
  under additional hypotheses, cyclic-sextic settings;
- the exceptional exponents \(1,2,3,4,6\) are also the
  crystallographic orders and the orders singled out by the classical
  integral-group-ring unit results associated with Higman;
- Shintani transfer is the mechanism in this program that reaches
  support orders eight and ten, and its cocycles are modular rather
  than group-ring constructions.

This suggests two provability regimes:

1. an **algebraic/crystallographic regime**, controlled by
   group-ring, index, and CM structure; and
2. a **modular/index-two regime**, controlled by Shintani cocycles,
   transfer, and height rigidity.

The current higher-order frontier would then be the complement of
their union. The proposed conceptual question is whether the repeated
appearance of the torsion orders associated with
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\), crystallographic groups, and integral
group rings reflects a common obstruction or is only a numerical
coincidence.

This framing must not be promoted from analogy until the precise
scope of Higman's theorem, the relevant group-ring minus parts, and
every known Stark proof mechanism have been checked. In particular,
"all algebraic methods stop here" is much stronger than the evidence
currently supports.

**First computation.** Build a row-by-row boundary matrix for every
proved or eligible higher-support census case:

\[
(\text{support exponent},\ \text{group-ring unit status},\
\text{Roblot scope},\ \text{CM scope},\
\text{Shintani index},\ \text{current verdict}).
\]

Test whether the apparent crystallographic/post-crystallographic split
survives the complete 2,704-row H stratum. One counterexample usefully
kills the strong framing; a clean separation motivates a focused
historical and structural paper.

### 6. Dedekind-sum control of the quartic Stark phase

Combine the quartic phase-quantization proposal above with the
Dedekind--Rademacher structure already present in the bridge
machinery. The working conjecture is that the defect

\[
  \arg L'(0,\chi)-\arg c
\]

for Roblot's canonical quartic solution admits a finite expression in
Dedekind--Rademacher sums attached to the relevant form, geodesic, and
multiplier data. If so, the condition that the defect lie in
\((\pi/2)\mathbb Z\) becomes an exact integrality congruence rather
than a transcendental phase assertion. Rademacher reciprocity would
then become a plausible proof tool.

This is the most promising collision because the program now has
certified, Artin-labeled quartic packets that can serve as exact
convention controls. The initial feasibility audit found that they are
**not yet genuine calibration responses**: the archived Engine-C
comparison vectors are obtained by Fourier inversion of the same
\(L'\)-data whose phase is to be explained. The active project at
`projects/dedekind-stark-phase` therefore requires an independently
constructed Roblot-canonical weak solution on the original
real-quadratic quartic extension before fitting. A fitted identity is
not evidence if it has enough free coefficients to interpolate the
controls, so the admissible formula family and its coefficient budget
remain frozen behind that independence gate.

**First computation.**

1. Reconstruct Roblot's canonical weak solution on each original
   cyclic-quartic extension and check (A1)--(A3) there.
2. Seal its coefficient before opening the archived packet or
   \(L'\)-value.
3. Freeze a low-complexity reciprocity-compatible formula family only
   after independent defects exist.
4. Fit against the independent certified defects.
5. Test the resulting formula, without refitting, on 50
   preregistered quartic census rows at 38 digits.

A clean failure is valuable: it identifies which phase information is
not contained in the classical multiplier data.

### 7. A product-formula pincer between Brumer--Stark and archimedean Stark

The speculative splice is to intersect two proved constraint systems:
finite-place Brumer--Stark information from the
Dasgupta--Kakde theorem and archimedean absolute-value/index
information of the kind supplied by Roblot. In a common overfield, a
global unit is constrained by its finite valuations, archimedean
absolute values, the product formula, and the unit lattice. Perhaps
their intersection recovers the missing phase or orientation in
selected rank-one archimedean cases without assuming Stark.

The likely obstruction is serious: the two constructions may live at
different moduli, in different fields, or encode incompatible local
objects. "Finite-place Stark is proved" is too broad a slogan and may
conceal exactly the missing comparison theorem. Accordingly, the
first task is not a general theorem but a controlled recovery test
where the answer is already certified.

**First computation.** Use RQ-001280 as ground truth. Choose and freeze
a small split prime, compute the relevant Dasgupta--Kakde-side
\(p\)-unit data in a pinned common overfield, and form the exact
valuation/unit-lattice intersection. Ask whether it recovers the
known \(\varepsilon\), its finite orbit, or nothing. Record separately:

- failure to place the two objects in a common ray field;
- a positive-dimensional lattice intersection;
- recovery up to torsion;
- exact recovery with orientation.

Only the last two outcomes justify formulating a reduction theorem.

### 8. Reverse the SIC bridge at the dimension-six wall

Instead of using Stark values to construct SIC data, use a published
exact dimension-six SIC fiducial to propose the blocked Stark value.
Under the AFK ghost correspondence, its exact overlap data should map
to the double-sine packet containing the order-six
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{21})\) wall.

This cannot prove the analytic Stark identity: the height-rigidity
argument still needs algebraicity or another theorem connecting the
analytic value to the algebraic candidate. Its value is diagnostic.
It could replace a diffuse conjecture about a zeta derivative by one
explicit target identity

\[
  X_A=\alpha,
\]

where \(\alpha\) is independently certified algebraic and obtained
from the SIC side.

**First computation.**

1. Freeze one exact dimension-six fiducial and its source/version.
2. Formalize the precise AFK ghost-map normalization and embedding
   convention.
3. Extract an exact candidate \(\alpha\) without consulting
   `bnrstark` or the existing Stark numerical value.
4. Compare the SIC-derived candidate and the double-sine route at 100
   digits.
5. Verify the candidate's field, polynomial, positivity/orientation,
   and predicted Artin orbit exactly.

Agreement would sharpen Paper III's closing hypothesis and test the
ghost correspondence in its most interesting unresolved dimension.
Disagreement would first trigger a convention audit, not an
after-the-fact relabeling.

### 9. Equidistribution of nonsplit Stark angles

At nonsplit archimedean embeddings, the powered packet components lie
on the unit circle. As the field and finite modulus vary, these give
Galois-labeled angle families. Once the quadratic census corpus is
complete, it becomes possible to ask whether these angles:

- equidistribute on the circle;
- have low-order Fourier bias controlled by genus theory or the sign
  class;
- exhibit correlations within Artin orbits;
- change distribution between primitive and imprimitive characters.

This would be a new empirical conjecture family rather than a claimed
consequence of Stark. Selection bias is the principal danger: only
angles from a completely solved stratum may be used for the first
distribution statement, and multiplicities must be reported both per
row and per distinct field.

**First computation.** After the exact Q corpus closes, preregister:

- the angle normalization and branch;
- row-weighted and field-weighted measures;
- the first 20 Fourier coefficients;
- stratification by conductor norm, sign class, genus data, and
  imprimitive Euler behavior;
- discrepancy statistics and a null simulation.

Compute these exactly where roots of unity suffice and otherwise with
certified enclosures. A visible bias generates a precise conjecture;
an essentially uniform result gives a quantitative null benchmark.

### 10. Export the dimension-six q-series wreckage

The failed dimension-six route contains identities that no longer
need Stark language:

\[
\text{Zak periodization}
\longrightarrow \text{lens beta integral}
\longrightarrow {}_2\psi_2
\longrightarrow \text{tilt independence}.
\]

The proof of tilt independence by Cauchy's theorem in a pole-free
strip, and any associated bilateral transformation, should be
extracted as basic-hypergeometric statements. They may be new, known
under an unexpected normalization, or recognizable as specializations
of Bailey/Slater transformations. Every outcome helps: a known
identity can import machinery back into the dimension-six wall, while
a new one is an independent theorem mined from the failed program.

**First computation.** Select the two or three identities with the
fewest Stark-specific parameters. For each:

1. write a standalone \(q\)-series statement with a precise domain of
   absolute convergence and pole exclusions;
2. derive it symbolically from the banked contour argument;
3. test random admissible complex parameters with Arb balls;
4. search the standard bilateral-transformation literature and
   transformation databases;
5. send the bare identities, not the Stark narrative, to a
   q-series expert.

Do not claim novelty until the normalization has been compared against
Bailey, Slater, and modern bilateral-series references.
