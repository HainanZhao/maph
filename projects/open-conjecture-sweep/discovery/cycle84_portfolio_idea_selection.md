## Question the inherited target

C81--C83 make poset majority graphs familiar, so continuing LEM, or choosing
the 1/3--2/3 conjecture merely because it also has linear extensions, risks
mistaking a convenient exact enumerator for information.  LRC has the
strongest finite interface, but fifty cycles of local enumeration, SAT, and
certificate variants have already failed its clarity gate.  Ryser and
Sidorenko each retain an open universal statement, yet their program-local
engines are also exhausted.  The relevant problem is therefore not "which
conjecture is easiest to enumerate?" but which target admits a **new** bridge
whose smallest exact failure changes a real strategic decision.

## Question that critique

This critique itself may over-penalize a target because the prior engine was
bad rather than the target.  It may also reward Frankl or 1/3--2/3 merely for
being fresh vocabulary.  A candidate must therefore expose a concrete
state-space map and a sharp small falsifier, rather than a generic finite
census.

## Independent candidate ranking

1. **1/3--2/3: minimal-counterexample separator/decomposition gate.**
   `CONJECTURED`: test whether a minimally unbalanced poset must admit a
   separator pair whose deletion components give a log-concave extension-ratio
   interpolation.  The verifier is an exact recurrence/injection proof; the
   falsifier is one exact finite poset satisfying the stated separator premise
   but violating the ratio inequality.  This is structurally distinct from
   C83 pair-majority cycle arguments.  Cost medium; information gain high.
2. **LRC(13): zonotope-to-sieve bridge.** `CONJECTURED`: use the 2026 zonotope
   formulation only if it yields a finite certificate that replaces, rather
   than accelerates, the paused p=199 local interface.  Falsifier: the bridge
   collapses to the existing prime sieve on a small control.  Cost high; a
   credible new route is not yet specified.
3. **Frankl: join-irreducible deficit transport.** `CONJECTURED`: turn a
   minimum-counterexample lattice condition into an injective deficit map.
   Falsifier: a small lattice satisfying that condition but violating the map.
   Cost medium-high; current mechanism underspecified.
4. **Ryser \(r=6\): inverse global-dual construction.** `CONJECTURED`: search
   for an exact dual certificate driven by the C72 blocker theorem.  Falsifier:
   a minimal symbolic model with unavoidable dual leakage.  Cost high; too
   close to the saturated defect framing.
