## Eligibility and creative comparison

The bounded 2026 screen leaves four candidates provisionally eligible:

1. **Sidorenko:** one S4 conjugacy-class T-transform for
   \(K_{5,5}\setminus C_{10}\).  Its exact polynomial has a sharp full-line
   sign falsifier and can expose a character-algebra bridge.
2. **1/3--2/3:** a substitution-prime two-cut blocking-ideal injection.
   Its sharp finite verifier is attractive, but its relationship to Gold and
   Peczarski certificates is not source-cleared.
3. **Intersecting Ryser at \(r=6\):** critical deletion-cover trace rigidity.
   The published \(\tau=5\) control could falsify an overstrong rule but says
   little directly about a hypothetical \(\tau=6\) system.
4. **Frankl:** lattice irreducible-deletion induction.  It risks collapsing
   into Bouchard's known minimum-counterexample conditions.

The inherited question, “can another finite host pass Zhao's comparison?”, is
misleading: C65's finite grids and C68's fixed-S3 theorem already rule out a
host census as progress.  Conversely, rejecting every computable S4 line
because it is finite would discard the first test of a representation-theoretic
transfer.  The decision question is therefore whether one *predicted*,
source-defined S4 class transfer has a reusable irreducible-character
factorization, not whether an additional row happens to be positive.

Oracle selected candidate 1.  The main rejected alternative is the poset
two-cut injection, because its overlap risk is unresolved.  The strongest flaw
is that a positive one-dimensional S4 row may be accidental and is far from
Zhao's all-group hypothesis or Sidorenko.  The expected information is high
about a possible character-theoretic lift of C68, and only moderate-to-low
about the target conjecture.
