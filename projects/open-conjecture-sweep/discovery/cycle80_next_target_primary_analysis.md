# C80 primary next-target analysis

## Question

Which remaining eligible problem has the best chance of a proof-grade advance
within the shared program, rather than merely the most familiar name?

## Question the question

The C76--C79 result shows that a recently stated, low-dimensional extension
can be tractable, but it also shows the danger of mistaking a preprint's proof
architecture for a new result. A candidate is not attractive merely because it
has a finite encoding or extensive prior computation: those can mean its
obvious engines are exhausted.

## Question the questioning

The preceding critique could overcorrect toward old, safely published
problems and miss a young theorem with a small exact interface. It could also
mistake a claimed 2026 preprint solution for a settled eligibility loss.
Every finalist therefore needs an exact-statement/current-primary check before
selection, not a search-result snippet.

## Current screen and candidate mechanisms

1. **Wilf, fixed multiplicity 19.** CORRECTED: Kliem--Stump, Discrete
   & Computational Geometry 67 (2022), Proposition 6.9, already proves
   \(m=19\). It is ineligible. Automatically advancing to \(m=20\) would
   inherit an expensive 200-GB face-enumeration method and needs a new screen.
2. **Intersecting Ryser at \(r=6\).** OBSERVED: the standard source still
   identifies the intersecting case as open from \(r=6\). The program already
   has a proved five-blocker boundary theorem but a stopped local ladder.
   Candidate mechanism: a genuinely new global cover/duality invariant, not
   another defect census. Falsifier: a valid \(r=6\) intersecting example
   with \(\tau>5\), or a structural countermodel to the proposed invariant.
   Strong flaw: this is a continuation-risk target, not a clean restart.
3. **1/3--2/3 poset conjecture.** OBSERVED: a March 2026 web-hosted
   claimed proof appears in a bounded screen, but its primary/published status
   is unresolved. It is ineligible until the claim is audited; do not attack
   it now.
4. **Quaternary Legendre pairs, length 42.** CORRECTED: the 2025 sources
   identify 42 as the smallest unresolved length; the prior construction-list
   reading was backwards. It has an exact construction/falsification interface
   but must begin with compression rather than a brute-force sequence search.

The official OpenAI screen was refreshed on 2026-08-05. It excludes the planar
unit-distance problem and all First Proof tasks; none of the two remaining
primary candidates is among those official outputs. This is an OBSERVED
eligibility screen, not a universal claim about all model work.
