# C81 second-engine selection: uniform-extension correlation

## Question

The dominance countermodel is compatible with generic reciprocal relations.
What property of the *uniform* distribution on a poset's linear extensions
could prevent a comparable edge from participating in a full 4-cycle without
an incomparable-only 4-cycle?

## Question the questioning

It would be easy to relabel the published order-14 census as evidence for an
inequality and repeat a smaller census. That has no universal leverage. The
opposite error is to treat the XYZ inequality as a universal answer merely
because it is a genuine uniform-extension theorem.

## Candidates

1. **XYZ/Four Functions composition (chosen).** The published XYZ inequality
   gives positive correlation of \(x<y\) and \(x<z\). Test, symbolically,
   whether any cyclic use of its common-source form forces a missing
   incomparable 4-cycle or only the known dominance chords.
2. **Chain-splitting construction.** Start from a known LEM triangle and
   replace one vertex by a comparable two-chain; derive the exact extension
   counts of the gadget. A successful full 4-cycle would refute Question 14.
   This is deferred until the correlation test identifies which comparison
   must be controlled.
3. **Finite poset enumeration.** Rejected: it duplicates Gupta's exhaustive
   evidence through 14 and supplies no missing bridge.

## Falsifier and advance rule

A concrete cyclic XYZ derivation that produces only the two dominance chords
rejects XYZ as the needed bridge; a derivation forcing an incomparable
4-cycle is a proof route. Any later gadget uses exact extension counts and
must either supply a real-poset counterexample or expose a new inequality.
