# C73 creative selection: low-hanging open-problem sweep

Candidate A — continue the intersecting Ryser equality classification. Its
decision question is whether every fully generalized defect-five core has a
five-vertex universal extension blocker. Falsifier: one core without such a
blocker. Rejected for this cycle: the state space expanded from a 16-orbit
submodel to 1,167 feasible partition pairs after two necessary corrections;
the present expected information gain is poor.

Candidate B — machine-generated graph-invariant inequalities. These have small
exact verifiers and a real chance of a finite counterexample. Falsifier: a
published refutation or a verified graph violating the statement. Chosen as the
primary class because it can yield a decisive result quickly without confusing a
bounded scan for a theorem.

Candidate C — celebrated finite construction cases, such as a smallest unknown
Hadamard order. Falsifier: an existing construction/nonexistence result.
Rejected as primary: verification is clean but construction search is large and
the chance of a new mechanism in one tranche is low.

Candidate D — continue the fixed-host Sidorenko route. Falsifier: a current
source showing the scoped result already known or a transfer obstruction.
Rejected: the fixed-S3 theorem is already at its scoped closure; a broader
transfer would repeat the same analytic barrier.

Question the questioning: selecting a famous conjecture because it is legible
confuses prominence with tractability. This cycle ranks targets by current
eligibility, smallest exact verifier, counterexample density, nearest theorem,
and the chance of a durable scoped result. The main rejected alternative is
another Ryser equality-core census; its falsifier is clear, but the framing
currently prevents us from seeing cheaper targets.
