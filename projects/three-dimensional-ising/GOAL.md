# Goal: structural representations of the three-dimensional Ising model

Investigate the nearest-neighbour, zero-field, ferromagnetic Ising model on
the three-dimensional simple cubic lattice. Do not initially guess a
closed-form critical temperature, and do not reproduce a purported exact
solution unless every identity is independently derived and verified.

## Primary objective

Discover an exact structural representation of the finite-lattice partition
function `Z_L(beta)` that could make the thermodynamic limit analytically
tractable. Possible directions include a higher-dimensional Kac--Ward or
Pfaffian construction, noncommutative or higher-form fermionization, a
commuting transfer family, a local encoding of domain-wall intersections and
topology, a substantive Ising/gauge-theory bridge, bounded or exactly
renormalizable tensor networks, a scoped no-go theorem, or a genuinely new
algebraic or topological structure.

## Required stages

1. Reconstruct precisely why two-dimensional solvability mechanisms fail in
   three dimensions, separating mathematical obstructions from historical
   assumptions.
2. Produce at least five genuinely different candidate representations. For
   each, give the proposed exact finite-lattice identity, transformed degrees
   of freedom, treatment of signs/intersections/boundaries/topology, the
   claimed reduction, and its first possible failure.
3. Select the strongest candidate by mathematical criteria and derive it from
   first principles.
4. Before any thermodynamic claim, verify exact enumeration on multiple
   finite graphs and cubic boxes, both temperature expansions, one- and
   two-dimensional reductions, free/periodic/antiperiodic boundaries, and
   positivity/analyticity or the harmlessness of intermediate signs.
5. Derive at least one new checkable consequence: an exact coefficient or
   recurrence, rigorous critical bound, relation among observables, smaller
   transfer representation, domain-wall theorem, or controlled free-energy
   route.

An integral, determinant, tensor contraction, or exponential sum is not an
exact solution unless it yields a demonstrated analytic or computational
reduction. Maintain an explicit failure ledger. A candidate that fails a
finite-size test is corrected by derivation or abandoned, never repaired by
assumption.

Major claims use the labels `PROVED`, `COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED`,
`CONJECTURED`, or `SPECULATIVE`. The success scale is:

1. new exact finite-lattice identity;
2. identity plus meaningful complexity or structural reduction;
3. controlled thermodynamic limit;
4. analytic determination of critical data;
5. complete accepted exact solution.

Begin with Stage 1. Do not claim that the model has been solved.
