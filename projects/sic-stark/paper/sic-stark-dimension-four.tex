\begin{abstract}
We study the rank condition that turns canonical
Shintani--Faddeev real-multiplication values into ghost projectors for
Weyl--Heisenberg symmetric informationally complete measurements.
We give exact exterior-square and parity-moment formulations
for the canonical family.  In dimension four, all $36$ minors of one
explicitly defined normalized ghost matrix
are divisible by the common factor
$x^2-\sqrt{3+\sqrt5}\,x+1$.  Using the Shintani--Kopp
Kronecker-limit theorem, we identify the modulus, labeled embeddings,
identity ray class, quarter characteristic, positive stabilizer,
multiplier, and partial-zeta sign.  The analytic class-number formula
for $\Q(\sqrt5,\sqrt\phi)$ then gives
$x^2=\phi+\sqrt\phi$.  Consequently the ghost has rank one.  The
identity-twist equation is the $\lambda=1$ TCC shift, and complex
conjugation proves the paired $\lambda=0$ shift.  Thus the formal
dimension-four Twisted Convolution Conjecture holds unconditionally.
\end{abstract}

\section{Canonical setup}

For $d\ge4$, put
\[
 Q_d=\langle1,1-d,1\rangle,\qquad
 L_d=\begin{pmatrix}d-1&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{disc}(Q_d)=(d+1)(d-3),\qquad
 L_d^2+L_d+I=dL_d,
\]
so $L_d^3\equiv I\pmod d$.  Its positive quadratic fixed point is
\[
 \beta_d=\frac{d-1+\sqrt{(d+1)(d-3)}}2.
\]

For $D_{p,q}=\tau^{pq}X^pZ^q$, $\tau=-e^{\pi i/d}$, write
\[
 P=\frac1d\sum_{\boldsymbol p}a_{\boldsymbol p}D_{\boldsymbol p}.
\]
The equation $P^2=P$ is a finite twisted-convolution equation for
the coefficients.  In even dimensions, the representative-wrap signs
of the displacement operators are retained.  The principal-ghost and
formal TCC constructions motivating this normalization are those of
Appleby--Flammia--Kopp~\cite{AFK}; software is not used as their
mathematical source.

\section{Finite rank certificates}

After a scalar shift, shear, and partial Fourier transform, canonical
twisted convolution is equivalent to rank one of a $d\times d$ matrix
$K_d$.  Thus it is equivalent to the vanishing of all $2$-minors.
For any complex matrix $K$,
\[
 \Delta_2(K)=\frac12\left((\Tr K^\dagger K)^2
 -\Tr((K^\dagger K)^2)\right)
 =\sum_{\substack{|I|=2\\|J|=2}}|\det K_{I,J}|^2.
\]
Hence $\Delta_2(K)=0$ if and only if $\operatorname{rank}K\le1$.

If a ghost matrix is denoted by $H$, it obeys
parity-Hermiticity $H^\dagger=PHP$.  Therefore
$R=PH$ is Hermitian and the rank-one certificate becomes
\[
 \Tr R^4=(\Tr R^2)^2.
\]
The fourth-moment identity is used only as an equivalent diagnostic
here; the dimension-four proof below uses the complete exact minor
certificate.

\section{Dimension four and conventions}

Let
\[
 \phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad \beta=\phi^2,\qquad
 \tau=-e^{\pi i/4}.
\]
To prevent convention ambiguity, define the function used here directly
by its gamma ratio:
\[
 S_2^{\mathrm{here}}(z\mid\omega_1,\omega_2)
 :=\frac{\Gamma_2(z\mid\omega_1,\omega_2)}
 {\Gamma_2(\omega_1+\omega_2-z\mid\omega_1,\omega_2)}.
\]
This definition is decisive.  Using Kopp's
$\operatorname{Sin}_{2,\mathrm{Kopp}}$ instead in the display defining
$x$ changes $x^2$ from $u$ to $4/u$, because the prefactor $\sqrt2$
is not inverted.

The normalized signed double-sine table is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt5&-x&1&-x^{-1}\\
-x^{-1}&-x^{-1}&-x^{-1}&-x\\
1&-x^{-1}&1&x\\
-x&-x^{-1}&x&-x
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[All sixteen principal overlaps]\label{lem:overlaptable}
The AFK candidate normalized overlaps for
$t=(4,1,\langle1,-3,1\rangle)$ are exactly the entries of the
displayed table.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 {\cal D}_{c,b}:=
 S_2^{\mathrm{here}}\!\left(\frac{c+b\beta}{4}\,\middle|\,\beta,1\right).
\]
The principal-form specialization of AFK Definition~1.32 is, for
$(p,q)\ne(0,0)$ and $r\equiv-p-q\pmod4$,
\[
 \widetilde\nu_{p,q}
 =(-1)^{\,4(p+q)+pq+\min(4,p+q)}
 {\cal D}_{4-p,q}{\cal D}_{4-r,p}{\cal D}_{4-q,r}.
\tag{DS}
\]
This is the three-factor cocycle decomposition for
$B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)^3$;
in particular it includes the AFK phase rather than appending an
unstated sign afterward.  The complete substitution audit is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
(p,q)&r&(-1)^E&
({4-p,q})({4-r,p})({4-q,r})\quad\longmapsto\quad
\widetilde\nu_{p,q}\\ \hline
(0,1)&3&-&41\,10\,33\mapsto-x\\
(0,2)&2&+&42\,20\,22\mapsto1\\
(0,3)&1&-&43\,30\,11\mapsto-x^{-1}\\
(1,0)&3&-&30\,11\,43\mapsto-x^{-1}\\
(1,1)&2&-&31\,21\,32\mapsto-x^{-1}\\
(1,2)&1&-&32\,31\,21\mapsto-x^{-1}\\
(1,3)&0&-&33\,41\,10\mapsto-x\\
(2,0)&2&+&20\,22\,42\mapsto1\\
(2,1)&1&-&21\,32\,31\mapsto-x^{-1}\\
(2,2)&0&+&22\,42\,20\mapsto1\\
(2,3)&3&+&23\,12\,13\mapsto x\\
(3,0)&1&-&10\,33\,41\mapsto-x\\
(3,1)&0&-&11\,43\,30\mapsto-x^{-1}\\
(3,2)&3&+&12\,13\,23\mapsto x\\
(3,3)&2&-&13\,23\,12\mapsto-x
\end{array}
\]
where, for example, $41\,10\,33$ means
${\cal D}_{4,1}{\cal D}_{1,0}{\cal D}_{3,3}$.
Every reduction in the last column uses only
\[
 S(z)S(\beta+1-z)=1,\qquad
 S(z+1)=2\sin(\pi z/\beta)S(z),\qquad
 S(z+\beta)=2\sin(\pi z)S(z),
\tag{FE}
\]
and $\beta^2=3\beta-1$; cyclically permuting the three displayed
factors changes nothing.  Thus the table is a finite check of
\textup{(DS)} rather than an appeal to numerical recognition.
At $(0,0)$ AFK's exceptional principal value is $\sqrt5$, completing
all sixteen entries.
\end{proof}

The zero characteristic is exceptional and is replaced by $\sqrt5$
before applying the displacement chirp.  Every table entry is then
divided by $\sqrt5$ in forming the normalized Weyl overlaps; in
particular the exceptional entry gives $a_{\boldsymbol0}=1$.

Define the matrix used in this paper, without reference to software, by
\[
 K_{r,c}(x)=\frac1{4\sqrt5}
 \sum_{\substack{0\le a,b<4\\r\equiv c+a\pmod4}}
 T_{a,b}(x)\tau^{ab}i^{bc},\qquad 0\le r,c<4,
\]
where $T(x)$ is the displayed table and
$\tau=-(1+i)/\sqrt2$.  Reduction of $r=c+a$ modulo four is performed
before evaluating the entry; the factor $\tau^{ab}$ retains the
even-dimensional displacement sign convention.  This formula is the
complete definition of the matrix whose minors are certified below.

\begin{lemma}[Weyl reconstruction]\label{lem:weyl}
Let $\nu_{a,b}=T_{a,b}(x)$ for the sixteen entries of the displayed
table, and put $a_{a,b}=\nu_{a,b}/\sqrt5$.  Thus
$a_{0,0}=1$.  With
\[
 D_{a,b}e_c=\tau^{ab}i^{bc}e_{c+a}\qquad(a,b,c\in\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z),
\]
the operator reconstructed from these sixteen normalized ghost
overlaps is
\[
 \Pi_I=\frac14\sum_{a,b\bmod4}a_{a,b}D_{a,b},
 \qquad
 (\Pi_I)_{r,c}=\frac1{4\sqrt5}
 \sum_{r\equiv c+a}T_{a,b}\tau^{ab}i^{bc}=K_{r,c}(x).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \Tr\Pi_I=\frac{T_{0,0}}{\sqrt5}=1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The displayed action of $D_{a,b}$ gives the matrix-entry formula
term by term.  On taking the trace, the sum over $c$ annihilates every
$b\ne0$, while $a=0$ is forced on the diagonal.  Hence only
$T_{0,0}=\sqrt5$ remains.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:minors}
Every one of the $36$ two-by-two minors of the dimension-four
matrix $K(x)$ lies in the principal ideal
\[
 \left(x^2-\sqrt{3+\sqrt5}\,x+1\right)
\]
of $\Q(i,\sqrt2,\sqrt5)[x,x^{-1}]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The exact Laurent expansion has $34$ formally nonzero minors and two
structurally zero minors.  Modulo $x^2-tx+1$, reduce each power using
\[
 x^{-1}=t-x,\quad x^2=tx-1,\quad
 t=\sqrt{3+\sqrt5}=\frac{\sqrt2+\sqrt{10}}2.
\]
Every remainder $A+Bx$ is zero.  More explicitly, for every row pair
$I$ and column pair $J$, the supplementary certificate records a
Laurent polynomial $M_{I,J}$ and a polynomial $Q_{I,J}$ satisfying
\[
 x^2M_{I,J}(x)
 =(x^2-tx+1)Q_{I,J}(x)
\]
coefficientwise in the genuine $\Q$-basis
\[
1,\sqrt2,\sqrt5,\sqrt{10}
\]
and separately in real and imaginary parts.  Thus the certificate is
verified by rational arithmetic only.  It contains all $36$ minors,
including the two zero quotients, rather than a Boolean summary.
\end{proof}

It remains to prove $x+x^{-1}=\sqrt{3+\sqrt5}$.  Shift and reflection
give
\[
 x=\sqrt2\,
 \frac{S_2^{\mathrm{here}}(\beta/4\mid\beta,1)
 S_2^{\mathrm{here}}(1/4\mid\beta,1)}
 {S_2^{\mathrm{here}}((\beta+1)/4\mid\beta,1)}.
\]

\section{The modulus-four ray field}

Let $K=\Q(\sqrt5)$ and $\mathcal O_K=\Z[\phi]$.  Since $2$ is inert,
there are $|(\mathcal O_K/4)^\times|=12$ finite residue units.
The images of $\{\pm\phi^n\}$, together with the sign at
$\infty_2$, have order $12$.  Since $h_K=1$, the ray exact sequence
therefore gives
\[
 |\operatorname{Cl}_{(4)\infty_2}(K)|=2.
\]
Label the embeddings by
$\infty_1(\sqrt5)=+\sqrt5$ and
$\infty_2(\sqrt5)=-\sqrt5$.  Set $L=K(\sqrt\phi)$.  Its relative
discriminant is $(4)$, and the second real place ramifies because
$\phi'<0$.  Hence $L$ is the full ray field of modulus
$(4)\infty_2$: the quadratic conductor--discriminant theorem gives its
conductor, and the previously computed ray group has order two.

Put $u=\phi+\sqrt\phi$, and define the nontrivial
$\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ by
$\sigma(\sqrt\phi)=-\sqrt\phi$.  Then
$u^\sigma=\phi-\sqrt\phi=u^{-1}$, so
\[
 N_{L/K}(u)=1,\qquad \Tr_{L/K}(u)=1+\sqrt5.
\]

\section{Class number, units, and regulator}

The field $L$ has signature $(2,1)$ and discriminant $-400$.
Its Minkowski bound is $15/(2\pi)<2.4$.  There is no ideal of norm two:
after establishing $\mathcal O_L=\Z[s]$, reduction of its defining
polynomial gives
\[
 X^4-X^2-1\equiv(X^2+X+1)^2\pmod2,
\]
so every prime above two has residue degree two and norm four.  Hence
$h_L=1$.

Write $s=\sqrt\phi$, so $s^4-s^2-1=0$.  The power-basis
discriminant is $-400$, equal to the certified field discriminant
below; hence $\mathcal O_L=\Z[s]$.  An exact Buchmann class-group/unit
computation in PARI/GP 2.15.4~\cite{PARI} returns fundamental units
$s^3-s$ and $-s^2+s$.  Since
\[
 s^3-s=s^{-1},\qquad -s^2+s=-u^{-1},
\]
these generate the same rank-two lattice as $s$ and $u=s^2+s$; the
exponent-change matrix has determinant one.  Consequently
\[
 \mathcal O_L^\times=\{\pm s^m u^n:m,n\in\Z\},\qquad
 R_L=\log\phi\,\log u.
\]
The exact computation also returns class number one, ray structures
$[2]$ and $[2,2]$, and the same regulator.  The output
\texttt{bnfcertify(B)=1} certifies the class group, regulator, and
fundamental-unit computation unconditionally.
The complete input and output are supplied in
\path{scripts/referee_pari_audit.gp} and
\path{certificates/pari-audit.txt}.

For the nontrivial quadratic character $\chi$ of $L/K$,
\[
 L(s,\chi)=\frac{\zeta_L(s)}{\zeta_K(s)}.
\]
For a number field $F$, the analytic class-number formula at zero is
\[
 \zeta_F(s)=-\frac{h_FR_F}{w_F}s^{r_1+r_2-1}
 +O(s^{r_1+r_2}).
\]
Since $h_K=h_L=1$, $w_K=w_L=2$, $R_K=\log\phi$, and the respective
orders of vanishing are one and two, division of these leading terms
yields
\[
 L'(0,\chi)=\log u.
\]

\section{The Kronecker-limit bridge}

Put
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}3&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}^{\!3}
 =\begin{pmatrix}21&-8\\8&-3\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \boldsymbol r=\binom0{1/4}.
\]
Take Kopp's modulus $\mathfrak m=4\mathcal O_K$, the identity ray class
$\mathfrak A$, $\mathfrak b=\mathcal O_K$, and $\alpha=4$.  Then
\[
 \mathfrak b\mathfrak m
 =4(\beta\Z+\Z),\qquad
 \alpha(r_2\beta-r_1)\mathcal O_K
 =\beta\mathcal O_K=\mathcal O_K\in\mathfrak A,
\]
and $r_2\beta'-r_1=\beta'/4>0$.  Thus these choices satisfy every
ideal and positivity hypothesis in Kopp's Theorem~1.1.

The matrix $B$ fixes $\beta$, belongs to
$\Gamma_{\boldsymbol r}$ because
$B\boldsymbol r-\boldsymbol r=(-2,-1)^{\mathsf T}$, and is the positive
generator of that stabilizer; its expanding eigenvalue is $\beta^3$.
The fiber above $\mathfrak A$ under
\[
\operatorname{Cl}_{(4)\infty_1\infty_2}(K)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}_{(4)\infty_2}(K)
\]
has order two, so Kopp's exponent is $n=2/2=1$.

Kopp's Definition~6.3 defines $\mathfrak R$ by the residue/sign pair
$(-1\bmod4,+\text{ at }\infty_2)$.  This is the nonidentity element
of the order-two ray group.  Indeed, every unit is $\pm\phi^n$.  For
$0\le n<6$,
the residues of $\phi^n=a_n+b_n\phi$ modulo $4$ are
\[
(a_n,b_n)=(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,2),(2,3),(3,1),
\]
and $\phi^6\equiv1\pmod4$.  Hence every unit congruent to $-1$ modulo
$4$ is $-\phi^{6k}$, whose value at $\infty_2$ is
$-(\phi')^{6k}<0$.  No global unit realizes Kopp's residue/sign pair,
so $\mathfrak R\ne\mathfrak A$; the ray group has order two, proving
the claim.
Hence, for its nontrivial quadratic character
$\chi$,
\[
 Z_{(4)\infty_2}(s,\mathfrak A)
 =\zeta(s,\mathfrak A)-\zeta(s,\mathfrak R\mathfrak A)
 =L(s,\chi).
\]
For $\boldsymbol p=(0,1)$, the Rademacher invariant of $B$ is zero:
$s(21,8)=-1/16$ gives
$18/8-3-12(-1/16)=0$.  The principal-ghost phase is
\[
 \Phi_{\boldsymbol p}
 =\xi_4^{-1}=e^{3\pi i/4},\qquad
 \Phi_{\boldsymbol p}^{\,2}=-i
 =(\psi^{-2}\chi_{\boldsymbol r}^{-1})(B).
\]
Explicitly, the zero Rademacher invariant gives $\psi^2(B)=1$.
Substitution into Kopp's defining theta-character formula (before any
even-denominator simplification) gives
\[
\chi_{\boldsymbol r}(B)
=\exp\!\left(2\pi i\,
\frac12\left(-\frac{28}{4}+\frac{152}{16}\right)\right)
=\exp(2\pi i\,5/4)=i.
\]
Thus $\chi_{\boldsymbol r}^{-1}(B)=-i$.  This is also exactly the
specialization of the phase relation of Appleby--Flammia--Kopp.

\begin{lemma}[Convention-matched cocycle value]\label{lem:cocycle}
For $\boldsymbol p=(0,1)$, AFK's phase and Kopp's modular cocycle give
\[
\nu_{\boldsymbol p}
=\Phi_{\boldsymbol p}\,
 \operatorname{shin}^{\boldsymbol r}_{B}(\beta)=-x,
\]
where, before shift/reflection simplification,
\[
x=\prod_{z\in\{1+\beta/4,\;1/4,\;1+(3\beta-1)/4\}}
 S_2^{\mathrm{here}}(z\mid\beta,1).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $C=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
so $B=C^3$.  Modulo four the characteristic orbit under the three
cocycle factors is
\[
 (0,1)\longmapsto(3,0)\longmapsto(1,3)\longmapsto(0,1).
\]
Before reduction, the applications of $C$ give
\[
 C(0,1)=(-1,0)=(3,0)+(-4,0),\quad
 C(3,0)=(9,3)=(1,3)+(8,0),\quad
 C(1,3)=(0,1).
\]
Insert these characteristics and translations in AFK Definition~1.32.
Equivalently, specialize formula \textup{(DS)} with
$p=0,q=1,r=3$.  Its exponent is
\[
4(p+q)+pq+\min(4,p+q)=4+0+1=5,
\]
and its three arguments are
$1+\beta/4$, $1/4$, and $1+(3\beta-1)/4$.
Kopp's $S$-generator formula uses
$\operatorname{Sin}_{2,\mathrm{Kopp}}
=\Gamma_2(\beta+1-z)/\Gamma_2(z)$, whereas our definition is its
reciprocal.  Formula \textup{(DS)} already combines the three
translation multipliers and $\Phi_{(0,1)}$ into $(-1)^5=-1$.
It therefore gives exactly the asserted signed product, with no
unrecorded phase.
\end{proof}

Because $S_2^{\mathrm{here}}$ is the reciprocal of the
Shintani--Kopp double sine, its shift and reflection identities give
\[
x=\sqrt2\,
\frac{S_2^{\mathrm{here}}(\beta/4\mid\beta,1)
S_2^{\mathrm{here}}(1/4\mid\beta,1)}
{S_2^{\mathrm{here}}((\beta+1)/4\mid\beta,1)}.
\]
Squaring Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle}, and using the multiplier computed
above, gives line by line
\[
x^2=\nu_{(0,1)}^2
=\Phi_{(0,1)}^2\operatorname{shin}^{\boldsymbol r}_{B}(\beta)^2
=(\psi^{-2}\chi_{\boldsymbol r}^{-1})(B)
  \operatorname{shin}^{\boldsymbol r}_{B}(\beta)^2
=\exp Z_{(4)\infty_2}'(0,\mathfrak A)
=\exp L'(0,\chi)=u.
\tag{KL}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Dimension-four rank theorem]\label{thm:reduction}
The explicitly defined matrix $K(x)$ has rank exactly one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \textup{(KL)}, $x^2=u$.  Since $u+u^{-1}=1+\sqrt5$,
\[
 (x+x^{-1})^2=3+\sqrt5.
\]
The defining reciprocal double-sine value is positive, hence
$x+x^{-1}=\sqrt{3+\sqrt5}$.  The common minor factor vanishes, so all
$36$ minors vanish by Proposition~\ref{prop:minors}.  Thus
$\operatorname{rank}K(x)\le1$.  Moreover, the explicit definition gives
\[
 K_{0,0}(x)=\frac{\sqrt5+1-x-x^{-1}}{4\sqrt5}
 =\frac{\sqrt5+1-\sqrt{3+\sqrt5}}{4\sqrt5}>0.
\]
Thus $K(x)\ne0$ internally, without invoking a ghost identification,
and its rank is exactly one.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The TCC coefficient identity]\label{lem:tccbridge}
For the canonical tuple
$t=(4,1,\langle1,-3,1\rangle)$, $\Pi_I^2=\Pi_I$ is exactly the
$\lambda=1$ instance of AFK equation~(1.49), not merely a rank
analogy.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand $\Pi_I^2$ using
$D_{\boldsymbol p}D_{\boldsymbol q}
=\tau^{\langle\boldsymbol p,\boldsymbol q\rangle}
D_{\boldsymbol p+\boldsymbol q}$ and compare the coefficient of each
$D_{\boldsymbol p}$.  Substitute
$a_{\boldsymbol q}=\nu_{\boldsymbol q}/\sqrt5$ and
$\nu_{\boldsymbol q}
=\Phi_{\boldsymbol q}\operatorname{shin}_{B}^{\boldsymbol q/4}(\beta)$.
The two identities used in AFK~\S5.4 specialize here to
\[
 \frac{\Phi_{\boldsymbol q}}{\Phi_{\boldsymbol q-\boldsymbol p}}
 =(-1)^{1+s_4(\boldsymbol p)}\tau^{Q(\boldsymbol p)}
   \tau^{(4-3)\langle\boldsymbol p,\boldsymbol q\rangle}
   \omega_4^{\langle\boldsymbol p,L_{z,t}\boldsymbol q\rangle},
\]
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{shin}_{A_t}^{\boldsymbol q/4}(\beta)}
 {\operatorname{shin}_{A_t}^{(\boldsymbol q-\boldsymbol p)/4}(\beta)}
 =
 \operatorname{shin}_{A_t}^{\boldsymbol q/4}(\beta)
 \operatorname{shin}_{A_t^{-1}}^{(\boldsymbol q-\boldsymbol p)/4}(\beta).
\]
For the identity twist, $f_t(1)=1$.  Its Weyl chirp supplies the
additional power $\tau^{\langle\boldsymbol p,\boldsymbol q\rangle}$;
therefore
$\tau^{(1+4-3)\langle\boldsymbol p,\boldsymbol q\rangle}
=\omega_4^{\langle\boldsymbol p,\boldsymbol q\rangle}$.
The coefficient of $D_{\boldsymbol p}$ is consequently a nonzero
factor independent of $\boldsymbol q$ times
\[
 \sum_{\boldsymbol q\in\mathcal I_{\boldsymbol p}}
 \omega_4^{\langle\boldsymbol p,(I+L_{z,t})\boldsymbol q\rangle}
 \operatorname{shin}_{A_t}^{\boldsymbol q/4}(\beta)
 \operatorname{shin}_{A_t^{-1}}^{(\boldsymbol q-\boldsymbol p)/4}(\beta)
 =16\delta^{(4)}_{\boldsymbol p,\boldsymbol0},
\]
where $\omega_4=e^{2\pi i/4}$ and $L_{z,t}$ is AFK's Zauner
generator.  This is equation~(1.49) with $r=1$ and $\lambda=1$.
Conversely the same coefficient comparison reverses every step, so
the equivalence is exact.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dimension-four TCC]\label{thm:shifts}
For every dimension-four admissible tuple $t$, one has
$0,1\in\mathcal Z_t$.  Moreover,
$\mathcal Z_{t'}=\mathcal Z_t$ for any two such tuples $t,t'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:weyl} identifies $\Pi_I=K(x)$ and gives
$\Tr K(x)=1$.  A rank-one
matrix satisfies $K^2=(\Tr K)K$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} gives $K(x)^2=K(x)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:tccbridge} therefore proves that $1$ is a shift for the
canonical tuple.  The twist--shift congruence is also explicit:
for $G=I$ it is
$2\lambda+7\equiv1\pmod8$, hence $\lambda\equiv1\pmod4$.

Complex conjugation sends the twist $G$ to $J_0G$, where
$J_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, preserves idempotence, and sends a shift
to
\[
\bar\lambda=-(\lambda+d_j-1)\pmod d.
\]
Here $d_j=d=4$, so $\bar\lambda=1-\lambda\pmod4$; consequently the
conjugate of the verified $\lambda=1$ equation is precisely the
$\lambda=0$ equation.  Equivalently $\det(J_0G)=-1$ in the
twist--shift congruence gives
$-(2\lambda+7)\equiv1\pmod8$, hence $\lambda\equiv0\pmod4$.

It remains to pass from the canonical form to every admissible form.
For $d=4$, admissibility forces $r=1$, $K=\Q(\sqrt5)$, and $j=m=1$.
Since $f_1=1$, every admissible form has conductor one and discriminant
five.  Such primitive indefinite forms constitute one proper
equivalence class: $h_K=1$ and the norm-$-1$ unit $\phi$ imply that
the narrow class number is also one.  Thus every form is $Q_M$ for
some $M\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\Z)$.  The transformation formulas of
AFK~\S7.1 change variables
$\boldsymbol q\mapsto\ell M\boldsymbol q$ in equation~(1.49), where
$\ell=\operatorname{sgn}j_{M^{-1}}(\beta)$, and give
$\mathcal Z_t\subseteq\mathcal Z_{t_M}$.  Applying the identical
argument to $M^{-1}$ gives the reverse inclusion.  Therefore both
verified shifts belong to every dimension-four admissible tuple.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and review points}

Theorems~\ref{thm:reduction} and~\ref{thm:shifts} prove the complete
dimension-four instance of the formal Twisted Convolution Conjecture.
They do not prove the Minimalist RM Values Conjecture, the Stark
conjecture, general TCC, or Zauner's conjecture in arbitrary dimension.
