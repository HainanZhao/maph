# SIC--Stark research cycle 27: one complex regulator remains

Date: 2026-07-27

## Outcome

The primitive dimension-six bridge has been reduced to one explicit complex
order-six regulator identity.  Everything else—including the ray generator,
Frobenius orientation, Euler factors, quadratic component, algebraic unit
orbit, and Fourier inversion—is now explicit.

Let

\[
 K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{21}),\qquad
 G=\operatorname{Cl}_{(6)\infty_2}(K)
   =\langle g\rangle\cong C_6,
\]

and let

\[
 \mathfrak p=(4\beta+1),\qquad N\mathfrak p=37.
\]

The exact ray logarithm is \([\mathfrak p]=g\).  If
\(\sigma=\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak p}\) denotes arithmetic Frobenius
and \(\varepsilon=x^2\), the certified algebraic orbit is

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
j&0&1&2&3&4&5\\ \hline
\sigma^j(x)
&x&-z^{-1}&-w^{-1}&x^{-1}&-z&-w,\\
\sigma^j(\varepsilon)
&x^2&z^{-2}&w^{-2}&x^{-2}&z^2&w^2.
\end{array}
\]

Write

\[
 r_j=\log|\sigma^j(\varepsilon)|.
\]

The remaining theorem is exactly

\[
\boxed{
 L_S'(0,\chi_1)
 =
 r_0+\zeta_6r_1+\zeta_6^2r_2,
 \qquad \chi_1(g)=\zeta_6.
}
\]

Its complex conjugate supplies \(\chi_5\).  The already-proved quadratic
identity supplies \(\chi_3\).  Fourier inversion then recovers all six
differenced ray-class derivatives and hence all three primitive
double-sine values in the required Artin order.

Thus the remaining obstruction is smaller than “prove the sextic Stark
conjecture” in an undifferentiated sense.  It is one convention-fixed,
one-character equality.

## 1. Correct composite-modulus Euler factors

PARI's `bnrL1` has two relevant modes:

- flag \(4\) returns the leading terms of the primitive \(L\)-functions
  together with their characters;
- flag \(6\) returns the \(S\)-imprimitive functions associated with the
  full ray modulus, again together with their characters.

The dimension-six Kopp formula uses the full modulus
\((6)\infty_2\), so flag \(6\) is required.

For the primitive order-six pair,

\[
 \frac{L_{S}'(0,\chi_1)}{L_{\mathrm{prim}}'(0,\chi_1)}
 =
 \frac{L_{S}'(0,\chi_5)}{L_{\mathrm{prim}}'(0,\chi_5)}
 =1.
\]

For the quadratic character,

\[
 \frac{L_{S}'(0,\chi_3)}
      {L_{\mathrm{prim}}'(0,\chi_3)}
 =2.
\]

This is the Euler factor at the prime over \(2\), absent from the
conductor-three primitive character but present in the modulus-six partial
zeta functions.  Numerically,

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_{\mathrm{prim}}'(0,\chi_3)
 &=0.86255462766206103218\ldots=\log Y,\\
L_S'(0,\chi_3)
 &=1.72510925532412206437\ldots=2\log Y.
\end{aligned}
\]

This correction is essential when reconstructing the full six-class
packet.  It does not alter the genuinely primitive order-six pair.

The lower identity also has a direct published source.  Shintani's
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{21})\), modulus-three example is precisely the product
of the three double sines used here.  In Shintani's/Kopp's convention its
value is \(Y^{-1/2}\); our reciprocal convention therefore gives
\(y=\sqrt Y\).  Tanaka reproduces the formula explicitly.  The source
search found no corresponding modulus-six evaluation of the primitive
order-six packet.

## 2. Fourier inversion of the analytic packet

Kopp's sign class is \(R=g^3\), so the group-ring difference \(1-R\)
annihilates the even characters and doubles the odd characters.  If

\[
 Z_j'=Z_{(6)\infty_2}'(0,g^j),
\]

then

\[
 Z_j'
 =
 \frac13
 \sum_{k\in\{1,3,5\}}
 \zeta_6^{-kj}L_S'(0,\chi_k).
\]

At 100 decimal digits, exact-character Fourier inversion of PARI's
independently evaluated Hecke leading terms gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
(Z_0',Z_1',Z_2',Z_3',Z_4',Z_5')
=(&1.58859444933534410745\ldots,\\
  &2.04648171295752176451\ldots,\\
  &2.18299651894629972143\ldots,\\
  &-1.58859444933534410745\ldots,\\
  &-2.04648171295752176451\ldots,\\
  &-2.18299651894629972143\ldots).
\end{aligned}
\]

These are exactly the expected logarithms

\[
\boxed{
(Z_0',Z_1',Z_2',Z_3',Z_4',Z_5')
=
(2\log x,-2\log z,-2\log w,
 -2\log x,2\log z,2\log w).
}
\]

The maximum residual at 100-digit working precision is below

\[
 4.6\cdot10^{-115}.
\]

This is a numerical audit, not an algebraicity proof.  Its value is that it
checks the modulus, Euler factors, ray generator, Artin direction, and
logarithm normalization simultaneously.

## 3. Exact algebraic decomposition

Let \(\sigma\) be the exact automorphism selected by arithmetic Frobenius at
\(\mathfrak p\).  Inside the degree-twelve field generated by \(x\), define

\[
 \varepsilon=x^2,\qquad
 Y=\frac{x\,\sigma^2(x)}{\sigma(x)}.
\]

Exact number-field reduction gives

\[
 \operatorname{minpoly}(Y)
 =Y^4-Y^3-3Y^2-Y+1,
\]

and recovers the base unit from the trace of the signed orbit:

\[
 \operatorname{minpoly}(\beta)=\beta^2-5\beta+1.
\]

Moreover,

\[
 Y^2-(\beta-2)Y+1=0
\]

inside the field, and

\[
 \prod_{j=0}^{5}\sigma^j(x)=1.
\]

The exact identity

\[
 Y=\frac{xz}{w}
\]

is therefore not merely a numerical norm relation.  On logarithms it gives

\[
 2\log Y=r_0-r_1+r_2.
\]

This is precisely the quadratic Fourier component

\[
 L_S'(0,\chi_3)=r_0-r_1+r_2,
\]

already proved unconditionally by the quadratic class-number calculation
and the Euler-factor correction above.

## 4. The remaining complex equality

The analytic primitive value is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda_1
 :=L_S'(0,\chi_1)
 ={}&1.52033704634095512899240833308247\ldots\\
 &+3.66283559358200064035435004970841\ldots\,i.
\end{aligned}
\]

The algebraic logarithmic resolvent is

\[
 \mathcal R_1(\varepsilon)
 :=r_0+\zeta_6r_1+\zeta_6^2r_2.
\]

Direct evaluation gives

\[
\Lambda_1-\mathcal R_1(\varepsilon)
 =
(1.5-1.0i)\cdot10^{-115}
\]

at 100-digit precision.

Proving

\[
 \Lambda_1=\mathcal R_1(\varepsilon)
\]

is exactly the primitive \(\chi_1\)-component of the rank-one abelian Stark
conjecture for this extension.  It is also sufficient:

1. complex conjugation gives the \(\chi_5\) equality;
2. the quadratic theorem gives the \(\chi_3\) equality;
3. Fourier inversion gives every \(Z_j'\);
4. Kopp's theorem converts these into the convention-correct squared
   double-sine values; and
5. the already-audited AFK phases select the signed square roots.

## 5. Why regulator and subfield formulas stop one step short

The intermediate-field class-number formulas and group determinants are
invariant under

\[
 \chi_1\longleftrightarrow\chi_5,
\qquad
 \sigma\longleftrightarrow\sigma^{-1}.
\]

They can constrain conjugation-invariant quantities such as products and
absolute values of the primitive character components.  They cannot choose
the sign of the inversion-odd component, equivalently the sign of the
imaginary part of \(\Lambda_1\).

The prime \(\mathfrak p=(4\beta+1)\) now fixes the algebraic orientation,
but a theorem is still needed to link that orientation to the analytic
Hecke value.  No further quotient of Dedekind zeta functions can do this,
because such quotients remain unchanged after simultaneously replacing
\(\chi_1\) by \(\chi_5\) and \(\sigma\) by \(\sigma^{-1}\).

This is the precise point at which the dimension-four and dimension-five
methods cease to generalize.

## Recommendation

The next proof attempt should target the boxed complex equality directly,
not the 225 minors and not the entire unit polynomial.

There are now two honest routes:

1. **Special-case analytic proof.** Expand \(L_S'(0,\chi_1)\) by the
   explicit Shintani cones for
   \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{21})\), reduce the resulting Barnes double-gamma
   expression using the period of
   \(\beta=(5+\sqrt{21})/2\), and compare it with the logarithmic
   resolvent of \(\varepsilon\).
2. **New wild-sextic theorem.** Prove a version of Roblot's
   absolute-value statement that retains the oriented
   \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_6)\)-component at the wildly ramified prime over \(3\).

The first route is narrower and should be attempted first.  A successful
proof would complete dimension six without developing a general theory of
wild cyclic-sextic Stark units.
