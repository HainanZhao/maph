## Exact gluing of the finite field

Let \(H\) be the degree-\(24\) field generated by a signed nonquadratic
overlap, and let \(C=\mathbf Q(\zeta_{56})\).  Their intersection has
degree \(12\).  Its relevant generator is

\[
 c=\zeta_{56}+\zeta_{56}^{-1},
\]

whose minimal polynomial is

\[
 T^{12}-12T^{10}+53T^8-104T^6+86T^4-24T^2+1.
\]

Sturm's theorem isolates the standard real value of \(c\) in
\((1.98,1.99)\).  Exact subfield inclusions select the corresponding
element \(c_H\in H\).  Among the \(12\) components returned for the
compositum, exactly one satisfies

\[
 c_H=\zeta_{56}+\zeta_{56}^{-1}.
\]

This exact equality replaces the former comparison of floating-point
complex roots.  In the selected field one also has

\[
 \sqrt2=\zeta_{56}^{7}+\zeta_{56}^{-7}.
\]

The two shifted Weyl reconstructions continue to satisfy

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}K=1,\qquad K^2=K,
\]

and every rank-two minor vanishes.

There are exactly \(12\) isomorphisms from \(H\) to this absolute ray

## Correction found in the Shintani audit

For an imaginary-quadratic divisor \(\mathfrak d\), Shintani's
distribution index is

\[
 n(S)=w(\mathfrak d)
 \frac{\lvert H_k(\mathfrak c)\rvert}
      {\lvert H_k(\mathfrak d)\rvert},
\qquad
 w(\mathfrak d)=
 \#\{\epsilon\in\mathcal O_k^\times:
 \epsilon\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak d}\}.
\]

The earlier script used \(w(\mathfrak d)=1\) for every nontrivial
divisor.  In \(k=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-7})\), the unit \(-1\) is congruent
to \(1\) for three nontrivial divisors supported to first order above
\(2\).  Computing the congruence as an exact ideal containment gives
four divisors with \(w=2\), including the trivial divisor.

This correction changes three individual clearing exponents but not
their least common multiple:

\[
 \operatorname{lcm}_{\mathfrak d\mid\mathfrak c}
 \bigl(12f_{\mathfrak d}n(S)\bigr)=16128.
\]

The real \(X\)-to-\(Y\) induction does not introduce products beyond
the audited full-to-submodulus indices, because along every nested
chain

\[
 \frac{|H(\mathfrak f)|}{|H(\mathfrak d)|}
 \frac{|H(\mathfrak d)|}{|H(\mathfrak e)|}
 =
 \frac{|H(\mathfrak f)|}{|H(\mathfrak e)|}.
\]

The source audit used Shintani's Proposition 4, Proposition 5, and
Lemma 8 in *On certain ray class invariants of real quadratic fields*,
J. Math. Soc. Japan 30 (1978), 139--167,
<https://doi.org/10.2969/jmsj/03010139>.

## Mathematical machinery that scales

The most useful general-purpose stack emerging from dimensions
\(4,5,7\) is:

1. **Character-first screening.**  Compute the exact ray character and
   its order before evaluating special functions.  Quadratic
   factorization gives the cheapest unconditional route; Shintani's
   index-two theorem is the next tractable case.

2. **Conductor lowering.**  Factor every characteristic contribution
   by its gcd with the nominal modulus.  This reveals smaller ray
   fields, reduces class-polynomial degrees, and often exposes
   reciprocal pairs.

3. **Normal-closure and intersection analysis.**  Compute commutators,
   maximal abelian subfields, imaginary-quadratic realizations, and
   conductors.  Conductor--discriminant constraints are more reliable
   than guessing fields from decimal polynomials.

4. **Certified analytic recognition.**  Use Arb quadrature, rational
   root isolation, and a height gap.  Numerical recognition proposes
   the polynomial; it never supplies the proof.

5. **Artin labeling by small split primes.**  Choose primes whose ray
   logs generate the class group and identify their automorphisms by
   local Frobenius congruences.  This turns an abstract field
   isomorphism into a characteristic-by-characteristic certificate.

6. **Exact intersection gluing.**  When phases and overlaps live in
   different fields, glue them through an exact common subfield
   generator.  This is preferable to selecting a compositum component
   by complex approximations.

7. **Orbit reduction before elimination.**  Apply Clifford/Zauner
   symmetry and reciprocity before Gröbner or resultant computations.
   In dimension seven this reduced \(48\) values to \(16\)
   representatives and then to eight independent positive quantities.

8. **Finite quotient-algebra verification.**  Once the field is known,
   verify idempotency and minors directly in a single exact number
   field.  Gröbner bases remain a fallback when a primitive-element
   collapse is unavailable.

9. **De-powering hierarchy.**  Use Voutier when the safe exponent and
   degree make its gap sufficient; otherwise use the regulator/unit-log
   lattice, and reserve Baker-type linear-form bounds for the genuinely
   difficult cases.
