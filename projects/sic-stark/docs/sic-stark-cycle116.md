# SIC--Stark research cycle 116: the 2026 coefficient-mutation theorem

Date: 2026-07-28

## New primary source

Ishibashi's *Cyclic Quantum Teichmüller Theory* was published in
*Communications in Mathematical Physics* on 9 July 2026:

<https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-026-05604-6>.

It gives an explicit finite-dimensional cyclic quantum dilogarithm,
proves its inversion and pentagon relations, and interprets the Fermat
parameters simultaneously as cluster coefficients and central
characters.  This is more directly relevant than the older
parameter-free pentagon formulations.

For odd \(N\), its operator is

\[
 \Psi_{\boldsymbol p}(X)
 =
 \prod_{j=0}^{N-1}
 (p^-+q^{2j+1}p^+X)^{j/N},
\qquad
(p^+)^N+(p^-)^N=1.
\]

Appendix B records the even-\(N\) modification with the necessary Weyl
signs.

## Application gate

The dimension-six geodesic supplies:

1. a valid odd subsequence;
2. exact central sectors \(z^n=\zeta_6^r\);
3. the standard cyclic normalization plus an explicit central factor;
4. the even-dimensional Weyl lift.

Therefore Ishibashi's coefficient language is the correct one for a
future operator match.  It does not by itself prove TCC: its pentagon
holds at one fixed order \(N\), whereas the Shintani modular cocycle
compares orders \(n\) and \(n'\).
