# SIC--Stark research cycle 129: the affine modular-gamma grid

Date: 2026-07-28

At the real-multiplication point,

\[
 \omega_1=\beta^3=24\beta-5,\qquad \omega_2=1,
\]

so

\[
 D=4\beta-1=\frac{\omega_1-\omega_2}{6}.
\]

Hence all \(36\) AFK samples form the affine grid

\[
 (\mu,h)
 =
 (\omega_2,-1)
 +a(0,-4)
 +b\left(\frac{\omega_1-\omega_2}{6},1\right).
\]

Its closure relations are

\[
 6(0,-4)=(0,-24),
\]

\[
 6\left(\frac{\omega_1-\omega_2}{6},1\right)
 =(\omega_1,5)-(\omega_2,-1).
\]

The right-hand vectors are exactly the two functional-equation shifts of
the general modular gamma. Their full rank-two image in
\(\mathbb R\times\mathbb Z/24\) is dense and must **not** be treated as
a topological lattice. The AFK grid only needs their difference

\[
 T=(\omega_1-\omega_2,6).
\]

The cyclic subgroup \(\langle T\rangle\) is discrete and cocompact.
Thus the characteristic set is an affine \((\mathbf Z/6)^2\) subgroup
of the legitimate helical quotient
\((\mathbb R\times\mathbb Z/24)/\langle T\rangle\), not an accidental
sparse sampling.
