# SIC--Stark research cycle 21: the ray square resolves the degree

Date: 2026-07-27

## Outcome

An apparent modulus mismatch has been resolved.

For a primitive characteristic \(r=q/4\), Kopp's denominator-ideal
construction gives the finite ray modulus \((4)\).  The corresponding
one-real-place ray group of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) has order two.

The double-sine cocycle value \(x\), however, is a square root of the
Stark invariant.  It is therefore \(x^2\), not \(x\), whose degree
should match the ray class field:

\[
\boxed{x^2\text{ has degree }2\text{ over }\mathbb Q(\sqrt5).}
\]

This is exactly what the candidate polynomial gives.  The degree-four
field generated by \(x\) is the square-root extension predicted by the
Shintani--Faddeev cocycle theorem.

The separately occurring modulus \(8=d(d-2)\) controls the modular
phase cover.  It should not be identified with the ray conductor
without an additional theorem.

## 1. Ray group modulo four

Let

\[
K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5),\qquad
\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\phi],\qquad \phi^2=\phi+1.
\]

Because \(2\) is inert, a residue \(a+b\phi\) modulo \(2^k\) is a unit
exactly when

\[
N(a+b\phi)=a^2+ab-b^2
\]

is odd.  Since \(K\) has class number one, the one-real-place ray group
can be enumerated from

\[
\mathcal O_K^\times\longrightarrow
(\mathcal O_K/4)^\times\times\{\pm1\}.
\]

There are \(12\) residue units.  The image of the global units also has
order \(12\), inside a signed group of order \(24\).  Hence

\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{Cl}_{(4)\infty_2}(K)|=2.}
\]

## 2. The square has the matching relative quadratic

Cycle 20 found

\[
x^4-(1+\sqrt5)x^2+1=0
\]

over \(K\).  Setting \(u=x^2\) gives

\[
\boxed{u^2-(1+\sqrt5)u+1=0.}
\]

Thus \(K(u)/K\) is quadratic, matching the order-two ray field.
Explicitly,

\[
u=\frac{1+\sqrt5+\sqrt{2+2\sqrt5}}2.
\]

The cocycle value \(x=\sqrt u\) then generates the further square-root
extension.  Its relative quartic is

\[
X^4-(1+\sqrt5)X^2+1,
\]

and its norm to \(\mathbb Q\) is

\[
X^8-2X^6-2X^4-2X^2+1.
\]

This degree pattern is precisely consistent with Kopp's theorem, which
relates RM cocycle values to square roots of Stark class invariants.

## 3. Why modulus eight appeared

The exact finite enumeration also gives

\[
|\operatorname{Cl}_{(8)\infty_2}(K)|=4,\qquad
\operatorname{Cl}_{(8)\infty_2}(K)\cong C_2\times C_2.
\]

That happens to match the degree of \(K(x)/K\), but it is not the right
reason for that degree.  Kopp defines the ray ideal from the
denominator of

\[
w=r_2\beta-r_1.
\]

For primitive \(r=q/4\), the denominator ideal is generically \((4)\).
The extra factor two belongs to the theta/metaplectic phase data and
to taking a square root of the Stark invariant.

## 4. New reduced target

The remaining analytic claim should now be stated for the square:

\[
\boxed{x^2=\frac{1+\sqrt5+\sqrt{2+2\sqrt5}}2.}
\]

The next calculation is to identify the nontrivial Artin conjugate of
the modulus-four ray field.  It should send

\[
x^2\longmapsto x^{-2}.
\]

If the Kronecker-limit formula supplies this conjugation and the norm
\(N_{K(u)/K}(u)=1\), only the relative trace

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{K(u)/K}(u)=1+\sqrt5
\]

remains to be proved.

## Claim ledger

Proved exactly:

- the modulus-four one-real-place ray group has order two;
- \(x^2\) has the matching relative quadratic polynomial;
- \(x\) is a compatible square-root extension;
- the modulus-eight ray group is a Vierergruppe, but its identification
  as the conductor was unjustified.

Still open:

- the class-field identification \(K(x^2)=K_{(4)\infty_2}\);
- the exact relative trace of the Stark invariant;
- the analytic double-sine evaluation and dimension-four TCC.
