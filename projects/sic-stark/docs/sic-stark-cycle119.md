# SIC--Stark research cycle 119: Heisenberg recovery of the second coordinate

Date: 2026-07-28

## Outcome

The fixed index-504 quotient alone is insufficient, but its Heisenberg
representation contains the missing six-dimensional phase space.
The key is to use an invariant sublattice, not the six-torsion subgroup
of the cyclic quotient.

Let

\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}5&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Multiplication by

\[
 2\sqrt{21}=4\beta-10
\]

is represented by

\[
 B=4L-10I=
\begin{pmatrix}10&-4\\4&-10\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det B=-84=-\frac{504}{6}.
\]

Since \(B\) is a polynomial in \(L\), it commutes with both \(L\) and
\(A=L^3\).  If \(X,Y\) are the Weyl operators at the two columns of
\(B\) in the level-504 Heisenberg representation, then

\[
 XY=\zeta_{504}^{-84}YX=\omega_6^{-1}YX.
\]

Their sixth powers commute.  The lattice \(6B\mathbf Z^2\) has Smith
invariants \((12,252)\), hence image order

\[
 \frac{504}{12}\frac{504}{252}=84.
\]

Every joint central-character space therefore has dimension

\[
 \frac{504}{84}=6.
\]

The trivial central character is fixed by \(L\) and \(A\), giving a
canonical invariant six-dimensional Weyl block.
