Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, “Dimension-saturating local
tomography of off-diagonal Fourier-interferometer errors from displaced
dark events,” for publication as a Regular Article in Physical Review A.

An ideal dark-event probability has no linear response to a small coherent
device error, so it cannot by itself support regular signed local
reconstruction. We show that positive and negative calibrated output probes
provide a reference amplitude that converts photon-counted dark events into
linear, phase-sensitive observables. Our principal result is an explicit
construction for every mode number \(m\geq2\): when \(n>2\) is divisible by
\(m\), a Fourier-cat input, \(m(m-1)/2\) dark outcomes, and two fixed probes
identify all \(m(m-1)\) off-diagonal Hermitian error coordinates locally.
The outcome and scalar-contrast counts attain their dimension lower bounds
within the weak-probe regular-local framework.

The paper also proves an exact number-state gauge obstruction, supplies
independently checkable rank certificates (including a sparse four-mode
determinant), bounds finite-probe bias, and gives the adjoint propagation
rule needed to map internal component errors to the reconstructed output
generator. The Fourier-cat suppression law and displaced-null principle are
known; the new contribution is their explicit bosonic identifiability
construction and its matching lower bounds, not a claim of a new suppression
law or global unitary tomography.

The revised manuscript explicitly compares the construction with
coherent-light and one-/two-photon transfer-matrix characterization. It
does not claim lower sample cost: for unit-spectral-norm probes the reference events
become exponentially rare with mode number. It provides the ideal
all-mode Cramér–Rao scaling, an A-optimal shot allocation, a weighted local
estimator with Monte Carlo validation, and a proof that unknown cat
amplitudes as well as phases are confounded with device errors.

The manuscript is self-contained, and exact symbolic programs, regression
tests, reproduction instructions, and a tagged code release accompany it.
