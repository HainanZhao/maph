## Dummit--Sands--Tangedal (2003)

In *Stark's conjecture in multi-quadratic extensions, revisited*,
JTNB 15 (2003), 83--97:

- Theorem 2 proves the refined equality `St'` for every
  multiquadratic extension.
- Theorem 4 proves the full Stark conjecture for every biquadratic
  extension.
- Theorem 1 and Theorem 3 give further full-conjecture cases under
  explicit conditions on (S), the distinguished place, and the
  multiquadratic extension.

Consequently this is genuine theorem-class antecedence for a quadratic
Engine-A quotient only after an exact comparison of the extension,
(S), distinguished place, normalization, and the claimed component.
The present census has not run that row-level hypothesis map.  It
therefore claims neither novelty nor automatic coverage for Engine A
from DST 2003.

## Roblot (2013)

In *Index formulae for Stark units and their solutions*, Pacific J.
Math. 266 (2013), 391--422:

- Theorem 5.5 treats quadratic extensions (the source identifies the
  underlying full result with Tate IV.5.4).
- Theorem 6.1 treats cyclic quartic extensions.
- Theorem 7.1 treats cyclic sextic extensions under its additional
  hypotheses.

The census's H taxonomy tests the exact eligibility conditions of
Theorem 7.1 for its sextic kernels.  Its 1,079 full weak-coverage rows
are a finite-range coverage count, not a new proof of Roblot's theorem
and not an Artin-labelled packet identification.

## Cohen--Roblot (2000)

*Computing the Hilbert class field of real quadratic fields*, Math.
Comp. 69 (2000), 1229--1244, studies (H_k/k), the maximal abelian
extension unramified at every finite and infinite place.  The promoted
objects here are one-place ray fields with nontrivial finite modulus.
They cannot be identified from a common base field alone.  The first
exact control is positive: the degree-32 B5-079 normal closure for
RQ-001262 over Q(sqrt(35)) has exactly one quartic subfield isomorphic
to Q(sqrt(5),sqrt(7)), its Hilbert field.  The replay records the
isomorphism.  This establishes field containment only, not equality of
Stark units or packets.  The remaining exact tasks concern
Q(sqrt(42)), Q(sqrt(51)), and Q(sqrt(186)).
