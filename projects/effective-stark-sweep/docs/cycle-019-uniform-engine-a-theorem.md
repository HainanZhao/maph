## Statement

Let \(K\) be real quadratic, let
\(\mathfrak m=\mathfrak f\infty_2\), and let \(R\) be the sign class
used in the differencing operator. Suppose every character satisfying
\(\chi(R)=-1\) has order two. For each such character \(\chi\), let
\(\chi^\circ\) be its primitive character, let \(L_\chi/K\) be the
quadratic extension cut out by \(\chi^\circ\), and let
\[
 I_\chi=[E_{L_\chi}/\mu(L_\chi):
   \langle E_K/\mu(K),u_\chi\rangle].
\]
Here \(u_\chi\) is the generator, oriented to be \(>1\) at the chosen
real embedding, of the primitive rank-one kernel of the exact norm map
on free unit lattices. Then
Then

\[
 L'_S(0,\chi)=
 E_\chi\,
 \frac{h_{L_\chi}}{h_K}
 \frac{w_K}{w_{L_\chi}}
 \frac{2}{I_\chi}\log|u_\chi|,
\]

where \(E_\chi\) is the exact product of the imprimitive Euler factors
at zero. Thus \(E_\chi=0\) if an omitted prime has
\(\chi^\circ(\mathfrak p)=1\), and otherwise it is the product of the
factors \(1-\chi^\circ(\mathfrak p)\), hence a power of two. If it
vanishes, that character contributes zero to the first derivative.
Fourier inversion gives
\[
 Z'_{\mathfrak m}(0,A)-Z'_{\mathfrak m}(0,RA)
 =\frac{2}{|G|}\sum_{\chi(R)=-1}
   \chi(A)^{-1}L'_{\mathfrak m}(0,\chi).
\]
All characters in this sum are quadratic, so the coefficients are
rational. Clearing their exact denominators proves a power identity
between each differenced invariant and an explicit product of
algebraic units.

## Why this is uniform

Because \(\chi\) is odd at the selected real place and trivial at the
other real place, \(L_\chi\) has signature \((2,1)\). Therefore
\(\zeta_K\) and \(\zeta_{L_\chi}\) vanish to orders one and two at zero.
The analytic class-number formula gives
\[
 \frac{\zeta_{L_\chi}(s)}{\zeta_K(s)}
 =
 \frac{h_{L_\chi}}{h_K}\frac{w_K}{w_{L_\chi}}
 \frac{R_{L_\chi}}{R_K}s+O(s^2).
\]
The imprimitive Euler factors give \(E_\chi\), and exact
ray-character Fourier inversion gives the displayed differencing
formula.

It remains only to prove the regulator quotient. In free unit
coordinates, let \(v_K\) be the embedded fundamental unit of \(K\) and
let \(v_{\rm rel}\) be a primitive generator of the kernel of the
exact norm map \(E_{L_\chi}/\mu(L_\chi)\to E_K/\mu(K)\). Then
\[
 I_\chi=|\det(v_K,v_{\rm rel})|.
\]
The two real embeddings of a relative unit are reciprocal, while the
complex place contributes twice in the standard regulator. The
determinant of the subgroup generated by \(E_K\) and \(u_\chi\) is
therefore \(2R_K\log|u_\chi|\). Since this subgroup has index
\(I_\chi\), its regulator is also \(I_\chi R_{L_\chi}\). Hence,
uniformly,

\[
 \frac{R_{L_\chi}}{R_K}=\frac{2}{I_\chi}\log|u_\chi|.
\]

This norm-kernel determinant is the definition used by the bulk
verifier. A bare gcd of the coordinates of the embedded base unit is
not accepted as a substitute: the relative vector must first be
derived as the primitive exact norm kernel.

Thus the theorem is single and uniform. What remains case-dependent is
finite verification data—primitive conductors, class numbers, Euler
factors, exact norm maps, unit-coordinate determinants, and
orientation—not a new proof.
