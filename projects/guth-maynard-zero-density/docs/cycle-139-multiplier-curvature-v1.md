# Cycle 139: multiplier curvature extends the low-edge range

On `|E_d|~J=X^j`, Cycle 138 lowers the exceptional multiplier height to

```text
H=N^2/J.                                           (1)
```

The approximation `|g^d-r_d|<<N^2/S^2` becomes a logarithmic-mode tube of
width

```text
delta=D N^2/S^2.                                  (2)
```

Apply the checked order-three theorem in the multiplier numerator and sum
the denominator block of length `H`.  Before edge weight, its four terms are

```text
D^(1/6) H^(3/2),
H^2 delta^(1/3),
H^2 (delta/D)^(1/3),
H.                                                 (3)
```

After multiplying by `J^2`, their exponents are

```text
derivative: 1/10+3rho+j/2,
tube:       1/5+14rho/3-2tau/3,
ratio:          14rho/3-2tau/3,
constant:   2rho+j.                               (4)
```

The edge-weight target is `2/3-2mu`.  Thus the two `j`-dependent conditions
are

```text
j<17/15-4mu-6rho,
j<2/3-2mu-2rho.                                   (5)
```

The derivative ceiling remains positive through

```text
rho<17/90-2mu/3.                                  (6)
```

Compared with the Cycle-138 all-edge ceiling, this gains

```text
(17/90-2mu/3)-(1/6-mu/2)
  =1/45-mu/6 >0.                                  (7)
```

At equality in (6), taking the minimal `tau=xi+1/3-rho`, the tube, ratio,
and constant margins are uniformly positive throughout the lower band.  The
endpoint itself ties in the derivative term and is excluded.

Order three therefore closes a strict new collection of low-edge cells, but
not all edge multiplicities: its derivative term grows like `j/2`, while the
constant term grows like `j`.  No high-edge or all-multiplicity extension,
full paired norm, endpoint, moment, density, or prime-interval theorem is
proved.
