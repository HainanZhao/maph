# Cycle 97 discovery candidate: algebraic roots or near-double roots

`CONJECTURED`: for

```text
f(t)=A-B exp(at)-C exp(bt)
```

with positive integer coefficients and a noncentral integer mode, clearing
negative powers from `P(Y)=f(log Y)` produces a nonzero integer trinomial.
Thus every exact real zero of `f` has algebraic exponential coordinate.

Since `f''<0`, a small nonzero value `f(x)` should have only two possible
explanations:

1. the local Newton step crosses a simple algebraic root; or
2. `f'(x)` is also small, giving a near-double-root certificate. When the
   latter certificate is sufficiently local, the unique critical point has
   explicit algebraic coordinate
   `(-Cb/(Ba))^(1/(a-b))`.

The degree, coefficient norm, Newton constants, and transition between the
two outputs must be frozen before this becomes a theorem.
