One issue is the almost counterexample we discussed in Section 5.5. Let MAC be the matrix
from the almost counterexample. Everything we wrote in this section so far applies equally to
MDir and MAC. In order to prove a bound for MDir which is stronger than the one for MAC, we
could try to prove a bound for ∥ ˜SMDir,r∥ which is much less than ∥ ˜SMAC,r∥ for some r. In time

∥ ˜SMDir,r∥ is much less than ∥ ˜SMAC,r∥. But for a proof, we still need to put our finger on a property
distinguishing MDir from MAC that could be used to bound these norms.
Eventually, Maynard and I did find a special property of MDir that distinguishes it from MAC
and we were able to use this to prove Theorem 6.1. We explain these ideas in the next section.

There are two cases depending on whether the set W has a lot of additive structure. Notice
